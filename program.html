Pushpanjali

Raagam: Gambheera Nattai  |  Talam: Adi

Dancers

Nritya

Musicians

Sanjana V., Manasa, Satya, Sweta

The opening act of our performance is the Pushpanjali, an energetic invocatory dance inspired by Maharaja Sri. B. Krishnadasar. Setting the tone as a sacred "offering of flowers", this piece marks the optimal beginning of our recital. You will feel the dedication of Nritya's performers as they make an offering to the divine and pray for blessings of the audience. Most importantly, don't miss the beautiful salutation to Bhumi (Mother Earth), asking for her blessings as they begin dancing upon her feet. This vibrant introduction offers a glimpse into the elegant and rhythmic beauty of one of our dancers, setting an auspicious and spectacular tone for the recital ahead.

Maathe

Raagam: Kannadaa  |  Talam: Adi

Dancers

Sofiya, Sunatha, Ananya, Rhea K, Sandra, Neera, Rachana, Prithika, Janani

Musicians

Sanjana V., Manasa, Satya, Sweta

Composed by Hariharasudha Maithri Bhagavathar and choreographed by Bharatanatyam Co-captain Nivedita, "Maathe Meenakshi" devises form in traditional format in a Varnam, and instead will be performed as a beautiful imagined "salutation of Goddess Parvati". This piece illustrates Parvati as the primordial Mother of the Universe, the source of the three Gunas, the manifestation of Golden Parvati, ruler of Lord Shiva. Drawing from Soundarya Lahari and her own divine imagination, she brings the Kamakshi from cool sanctuaries of Mother Meenakshi and Kamakshi River into one, casting her in the "Life Force" residing within all living beings and the very center of the cosmos. This spirituality is matched by intricate footwork and formations during the Swaram passage, blending devotion with rhythmic precision.

Musicians

Bhuvanitha, Mahika, Sanjana K, Maanyav, Satya, Sanjana V

Composed by S. N. Omanakuippa Pillai, the Parandhamane is a popular devotional piece has been reimagined and structured as a Parandhamane for this show, allowing our audiences to see the full depth of both technical rhythm and supreme storytelling. As hits the inspiring stories of each stanza, React the protecting divinity of Rama and his unwavering Ram and his saintly forms and the various virtuous figures through breathtaking footwork and intricate formations, it is a true testament to the team's synchronicity, showcasing the balance of strength and grace as we celebrate the presence of the universe.

Madhurashtakam

Raagam: Kambhoji  |  Talam: Khadi

Dancers

Gayathri, Anurag

Choreographed by Guru Pankaj Chaturvedi, the Madhurashtakam journeys the childhood of Lord Krishna through the infinity of the mother's love. Each stanza is a different moment of Krishna's life, from his birth in his playful, mischievous youth, filled with adoration, each move imbued with his playful, mischievous and beautiful smile. His charming nature unfold, as charming stories unfold, celebrating Krishna as a divine symbol of love and devotion.

Thillana

Talam: Adi

Dancers

Nivedita

Musicians

Sweta, Maanyav

A thillana traditionally serves as the high-energy finale of a Bharatanatyam recital, and what makes this one extra special is it has been composed by Dr. M. Balamurali, one of the greatest Carnatic musicians and innovators of the 20th century. Composed by Dr. Harinisridambara, and choreographed by Nivedita, working and innovating from her fellow musicians and vocalists, the Thillana introduces a combined conceptual layer in the philosophy of pure movement and rhythm. Unlike other thillanas, with an intricate weaving and interweaving movements, the dancer depicts the magic and motion of a Majestic Eagle in the middle of a full flight. The rhythm, at times, fills the stage and becomes the ground dance in addition to the rhythmic footwork — as she transcends the sage and hints the general dance divine. As the dance crescendos its music and dance elevate together, it passes becomes a final act of devotion. This piece is more than a technical showpiece; it is a glorious celebration of the art form, honoring the divine source of dance at the core and celebrating the present journey of dance at CWRU.

Mangalam

Raagam: Saurashtra  |  Talam: Adi

Dancers

NRITYA

Musicians

Gayathri, Anurag

This performance closes with a sacred close dance called the Mangalam, a prayer of gratitude to the divine, the audience, the musicians, and the orchestra. Just as the Pushpanjali opened our journey into the spiritual world created by our art forms and imagination, the Mangalam is our humble and heartfelt farewell, expressing the pure and deepest thankfulness. The performance concludes by celebrating the journey, honoring the stage, the art form, and the community that has brought us all here. In conclusion, the beautiful melody of Saurashtra weaves the ensemble into a unified whole, leaving the stage with a sense of peace and universal blessing.
