Program Details

Seating starts at 2:30 PM. Event starts at 3 PM.

Ithihasa Anubhava, an "Experience of the Epics" is a unique collaboration between CWRU's Indian Classical Music and Indian Classical Dance teams. With dance styles spanning Bharatanatyam, Kuchipudi, Kathak, and Odissi, with Carnatic and Hindustani orchestras, we pay homage to well-known, traditional epics of Durga Devi, Lord Vishnu and Lord Shiva. We are very excited to introduce you to the world of Indian classical aesthetics through an incredibly special night of music & dance. Sit back, relax, and enjoy the show!

Pushpanjali
Raagam: Ghambeeranatai  |  Talam: Aadi

The opening act of our performance is the Pushpanjali, an energetic invocatory dance composed by Madurai Sri R. Muralidharan. Serving as a sacred "offering of flowers," this piece marks the spiritual beginning of our recital. You will witness the dancers perform their Namaskaram, a ritual of deep respect where they seek the blessings of the divine, their Guru, and the audience. Most importantly, they offer a humble salutation to Bhumi Mata (Mother Earth), asking for her grace as they begin dancing upon her. This vibrant introduction offers a glimpse into the diverse styles and rhythmic intensity of our dance, setting an auspicious and spirited tone for the recital ahead.

Maathe
Raagam: Kamas  |  Talam: Aadi

Composed by Harikesanallur Muthiah Bhagavatar and choreographed by Bharatanatyam Co-captain Nivedita, Maathe Malayadhwaja deviates from its traditional format as a Varnam, and instead will be performed as a beautiful Keerthanam in praise of Goddess Parvati, the consort of Lord Shiva.

This piece illustrates Parvati as the primordial Mother of the Universe. Through a short storytelling, the dancers depict the miraculous origins of her children: the creation of Ganesha from sandalwood and her own divine imagination, and the formation of Murugan as she gathered six infants from the lotus blossoms of the Saravana River into one.

The Keerthanam reaches a powerful ending, hailing her as the "Life Force" residing within all living beings and the very center of the cosmos. This spirituality is matched by intricate footwork and formations during the Swaram passages, blending devotion with rhythmic precision.

Parandhamane
Raagamalika  |  Talam: Aadi

Composed by K.N. Dandayudhapani Pillai, this Dashavatara Kriti is a popular devotional piece reimagined and structured as a Padavarnam for this performance, allowing our team to showcase the full depth of both technical rhythm and expressive storytelling.

This Varnam serves as a tribute to Lord Vishnu, taking the audience on a spiritual journey through his Dashavataras (ten incarnations). Through evocative sancharis, the dancers bring to life the inspiring stories of each avatar, from the protective Matsya to the righteous Rama and the mysterious Krishna, pausing only before the final avatar, Kalki, who is yet to manifest. The dance weaves together soulful expressions with breathtakingly fast and intricate footwork; it is a true testament to the team's synchronicity, showcasing the balance of strength and grace as we celebrate the preserver of the universe.

Madhurashtakam
Raagamalika  |  Talam: Ektali

Choreographed by Guru Pankaj Charan Das, Madhurastakam portrays the childhood of Lord Krishna through the loving eyes of his mother, Yashoda. The word madhuram means "sweet," reflecting how every moment of Krishna's life, from his smiles to his playful mischief, is filled with joy in her eyes. As Krishna grows from an infant into a lively young boy, his charming antics unfold, including his love for butter and playful moments with his friends. The piece concludes in devotion, celebrating Krishna as a divine symbol of love and compassion.

Marakata Mani Maya Chela
Raagam: Aarabhi  |  Talam: Aadi

Choreographed by Padmabhushans Gurus Dr. Raja Radha Reddy and reworked by Minaakshi, this item is a tarangam meaning "waves". With complex yet delicate footwork and melodious abhinaya, the dance describes Sri Krishna in all his glory. He is hailed as the emerald-adorned beauty with lotus like eyes, the bestower of salvation and the protector of the universe. One of the highlights of the dance is a reference to "Rasaleela," or the famous divine dance of love between Radha and Krishna. The item concludes with the dancer balancing on the rim of a brass plate, performing intricate footwork in tandem with the orchestra. The plate dance signifies the spiritual unison between dancer and Bhudevi, where the dancer lets go of all other materialistic possessions and is one with the earth.

Thillana
Raagam: Hindolam  |  Talam: Aadi

A thillana traditionally serves as the high-energy finale of a Bharatanatyam margam, this ending is even more special as it serves as a final milestone by the graduating seniors of both Naadam and Nritya.

Composed by Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna and choreographed by Nivedita with creative input from her fellow seniors in the music ensemble, this Thillana introduces a profound conceptual layer: the philosophy of Ardhanareshwara. Through seamless transitions and mirroring movements, the dancer depicts the singular, inseparable union of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati, the perfect harmony of Purusha (energy) and Prakriti (nature) using tandava, the rigid and lasya the graceful dance forms. As the dancer invokes Lord Nataraja, the King of Dance, the fast-paced footwork and poses become a final act of devotion. This piece is more than a conclusion; it is a collective bow of gratitude from the graduates, honoring the divine source of their art and celebrating the journey they have shared here at CWRU.

Mangalam
Raagamalika  |  Talam: Aadi

The performance draws to a sacred close with the Mangalam, a traditional concluding prayer that serves as a humble gesture of gratitude to the universe, the divine, our gurus, and the orchestra. This final piece honors the Rasikas, our audience, whose presence and immersion allow them to travel into the spiritual world created by the performers and musicians. Set to the soul-stirring bhajan Garuda Gamana and followed by the auspicious shloka Mangalam Kosalendraya invoking the grace of Lord Rama, the dancers conclude the event by performing their final Namaskaram in unison. This symbolic offering marks the completion of the spiritual circle, as we bow down to the Lord of Dance and the community that has shared in this journey, leaving the stage with a sense of peace and universal blessing.
